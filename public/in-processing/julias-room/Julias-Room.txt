Julia's Room was a gallery exhibition by Dave Hannon, artist and fellow PhD researcher in Digital Media at Drexel University, presented at Pearlstein Gallery. The work engages queer world-building through the avatar Julia, a character Dave developed to reconstruct a narrative about growing up queer in a heteronormative environment. The exhibition brought together sculpture, projection, augmented reality, and interactive digital performance to fabricate alternative queer spaces and explore themes of queer failure and domestic intimacy.
I collaborated on the technical production of the show alongside Nicolas Losada and Arefeh Ahmadi, both also PhD students in Digital Media at Drexel.
My primary contribution was the full AR development for two sculptural pieces. For The Goop, a sculpture of wood strips and model magic resembling an ad hoc oil rig, I built the AR application and authored all the activation visuals: bright pink liquid spewing from the rigs out of the ground, with a rotating motor element, all rendered in AR layered over the physical sculpture. For Sponge, a large foam piece fabricated to resemble a giant kitchen sponge, I developed the AR bubble animation that would appear through an iPad, with bubbles rising from the sponge potentially carrying embedded imagery from the performance or room.
I also assisted Dave in developing the TouchDesigner pipeline for Just Julia, the eight-foot tall mirror piece. That work used a Kinect and live video feed with hand tracking to project digitally authored facial features onto a viewer's face in real time, running as a kind of digital puppet. My involvement was in the TouchDesigner side of that system.
Beyond the individual pieces, I helped with the physical setup and calibration of projection for the miniature, a handbuilt scale model of Julia's room that served as a source work for much of the larger installation. I also contributed to the broader logistics of planning and mounting the show in the gallery space.
