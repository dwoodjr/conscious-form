*Buffalo Soldier* is a wooden box of soil and rock with piezoelectric sensors and an electret microphone pressed into the material. Striking, pressing or dragging a hand through the earth sets off vibrations, and the sensors pass them to Max/MSP. It is Movement I, EARTH, of *Organic Memory (Triptych)*, the three-instrument composition at the center of my dissertation.

## The question

Picture a Buffalo Soldier meeting an American bison, and the two recognizing each other. Western expansion studied both in order to exploit them. The US Army sent Black regiments west against Indigenous nations while the same expansion drove the bison close to extinction, so each was weapon and target at once. Even the name binds them: by most accounts, the Indigenous people the regiments fought gave it to them.

My way in was my father's house. He collected the history and the images of Buffalo Soldiers and Black cowboys, and the memorabilia on his walls tells the doubled story the instrument is about.

## Building it

My first Max patch put the sensor signal through standard reverb, and the failure was instructive. Reverb builds an acoustic space with no memory, one that could be anywhere. The soil in this box comes from somewhere, so the processing has to keep its grit; granular processing is the direction I am taking now.

Sensitivity is the hardest constraint so far. With the piezo threshold set low, ambient vibration fires the instrument. Set it high and the subtle gestures, a finger drawn through soil, register nothing.

In EARTH the shared rhythmic cell from *When Johnny Comes Marching Home* slows to a geological tempo, one beat every four to eight seconds.
